Our process

Two people on your search, start to finish.

A subject matter expert who has led AI inside a business like yours, and a relationship manager who looks after both you and the candidates and makes sure the fit is genuinely right on both sides. First call to shortlist usually runs two to three weeks.

Week one

The honest brief

Not the job description. What has to be true in twelve months, what has already been tried, and who inside the business this person has to win over.

Week one to two

Names, not applications

We go straight to the people already doing this well in your industry. You see who we're approaching, and why, before anyone is contacted.

Week two to three

Interviews that go deeper

Your expert takes every finalist through situations they have lived themselves, then tells you what each answer actually revealed.

Roles we place

Which AI leader you need is worth thinking through carefully.

There are four common shapes for a first AI hire, and they are not interchangeable. Working out which one fits where you are today is often the most useful half-hour of the process.

Where you are

A few experiments running, and nobody really owns them.

Who to hire Head of Applied AI

Focus the work on the few ideas with a business owner and a number attached, stop the rest, and set a baseline you can measure against.

Where you are

The demos go down well, but nothing is actually live.

Who to hire Director of AI Engineering

Take one job people do every day and get it fully live, with someone's name on it, a way to switch it off, and a cost agreed up front.

Where you are

One thing works, and now every department wants it.

Who to hire VP of Artificial Intelligence

Turn that success into something other teams can reuse and run themselves. Half the job is technical; the other half is getting people to change how they work.

Where you are

The business would notice straight away if AI stopped.

Who to hire Chief AI Officer

Own AI the way a CFO owns money: know what's running, what it's exposed to, and have the authority to switch something off.
